# Safety — zero exceptions

What keeps an anonymous writer unnamed, a blocked person unreached and the store review passed. None of it is a preference: a change here re-checks it on the server and in `test/emulator`.

## Safety (App Store 1.2 / 5.1.1(v))

- **Blocking**: `users/{uid}/blocks/{blockedUid}` (owner-only). `blockedBetween()` in the rules refuses connections, messages, invites, notes, notifications and card links across a block, both directions; blocking also deletes the connection. Client reads drop blocked authors in `lib/data/hooks` (`getMyBlockedIds`).
- **Blocks hide people and their named cards, never anonymous cards**: the reader writes their own block list, so any answer about an anonymous card that changed when they blocked someone would name its author (block a guess, ask, unblock, next).
  Every server read keeps an anonymous card whoever the viewer blocked (`blockHides` in `present.ts`: the card, `?keys=`, feeds, a card's lists, card box shelves); the rules let a resonance draft answer one across a block (`originalReadable`); and what is aimed at one across a block — a note, a resonance, publishing an answer — is accepted and silently not delivered: the note is kept as its writer's own but addressed to no one (`toUserId: null`, no bell, no push) and names whom it was withheld from (`withheldFor`, read by no client, never exported), the resonance points at it and reaches no one.
  Every path asks the pen name before the blocks; a message's `noteRef` must be a note its recipient left the sender, one error otherwise, and one on the sender's anonymous card is left off the message (as is one gone with its card); the export keeps a note's words, card and date, never whom it reached or whether it was read — every note alike, since any difference between two would be a tell; deleting a published card — the server's alone, the rules let a client delete drafts only — deletes its notes after it; the account purge deletes the notes left on the account's cards just before each card (`byCard` in `collectAccountData`: a purge cut short never strands one) and those on cards gone earlier by `toUserId` or `withheldFor`, so the withheld go with the delivered.
  A note or resonance bell on an anonymous card says so (`payload.anonymous`) and its push opens the card, never the writer's thread (and so does its row in the web bell — see Push).
  The recommender lifts an author's cards for a reader who answered them only when the card is named (read as it is now, `namedCardIds`), counts only answers their originals show (published, public, named), and `/api/recommend/feed` answers card ids and reasons, never scores.
  Round 26: what a reader does on an anonymous card feeds only their taste in content — the +0.15 lift for an author they answered and the cold start's connections' cards first count named cards alone, judged as they are when the picks are built; the taste is made only of cards the reader may read now (public, their own, a connection's connections-only card: a bookmark is a client write that may name any card id); a reason never says what the reader did (read, saved, resonated, "you saved…") nor names a sensitive situation (`REASON_RULE` in the select prompt, and `reasonSafe` before a stored reason is pushed); the dry run and `nearest.ts` name no author and write nothing (`report.test.ts` scans them).
  An anonymous card is public or private, never for connections only: whether a reader could open one would turn on a connection, and a block ends the connection — the card vanishing would name its author.
  The rules (`anonymousForConnections`) refuse a new draft, an update of a published card's `anonymous`/`visibility` (an unpublished draft may be so, for the builds before letters — publishing refuses it) and a pending edit (`editedAudience`: the edit's visibility, else the card's) that would make one so; publishing, PATCH and edits/apply refuse it with 400 'An anonymous card is public or private.' (`lib/db/firestore/cardContent.ts`); a card already that way stays editable in everything else, is read as private by everyone else (`cardVisible`, the rules' `cardOpen`), and `scripts/integrity.ts` lists the published ones — never migrate them.
  `test/emulator/anonymousBlocks.emulator.test.ts` asks each probe with and without a block, either way.
  Named cards keep every block behaviour.
- **Trash** (`src/lib/api/v1/trash.ts`, the clock in `src/lib/cards/trash.ts`: `TRASH_DAYS` 30, the purge date computed, never stored): a card in the trash is private to everyone but its author — every public query, the rules' reads, `?keys=` (its author too: the thought map's fallback and a thread's card read alike to both), embeds, notes, resonances, reports, picks, pushes and the sitemap treat it as a private card already does, so a trashed anonymous card answers every probe exactly as a private anonymous one, with and without a block either way (`anonymousBlocks.emulator.test.ts`) — and what answers anyone without a viewer (the card page's server render and so `/api/cards/view`, `/api/cards/resolve`) goes further and answers as for a deleted card (`cardByKeyForAnyone`: `{ id: null }`, not a private card's id): the document outlives the trash's moment until its 30 days or its author's account run out, and an anonymous card turning from "there" to "gone" in the purge's minute would say whose it was (its author finds it through GET /api/v1/cards/{key}; what is left is its slug, still held, so a new card wanting it gets a suffix) — and `cardVisible` backs it up (anyone else: false; its owner: until its purge date, after which it is gone to them too, before the cron).
  It answers nothing while there (`referenceCardId` parked in `trashedFrom`, so every one-per-reader reader ignores it, installed builds too); trashing a resonance takes it back at once, and its connection with it when nothing else holds it — a restore never brings that back (the bell is the record: it rings once ever), which is why the card ⋯ asks first for a published, public, named resonance whose original is named (plain colour; red is for 永久刪除 and 清空 only).
  An anonymous card's notes are deleted as it goes in, as deleting it deletes them: kept until the 30-day purge, "trash, then delete the account" would make them vanish with the author's profile (a resent `clientId` answers 201 while the note exists, 404 after) and say whose card it was; a named card keeps its notes.
  The rules refuse every client update of a trashed card and every create/update of its `edits/current` (`resource.data.trashedAt` / `card().trashedAt`), and no client writes `trashedAt`/`trashedFrom`; a client may still delete a trashed draft (older tester builds), never a trashed published card.
  Every refusal comes after the ownership check, inside the transaction (a stranger gets a missing card's 404 for any trashed id); a second trash writes nothing; `deleteCard`'s `trashedBefore` (the cron, emptying) and `onlyIfTrashed` (the trash's 永久刪除) keep a card restored meanwhile.
  A trashed card can't be attached to a message, even by its author, and a `noteRef` on the sender's own trashed card counts as gone with it.
  The account purge takes trashed cards as any card of the author's (`collectAccountData`), but names none of their pages to revalidate (they went with the trash; starting their cache again beside the profile's would tie an anonymous one to it); the export keeps them as stored.
- **Deleting a conversation**: through the server, whole (`DELETE /api/v1/conversations/{pair}`: either participant; anyone else, whether it exists or not, a 404; messages before the conversation; `letters/*` kept) — the web and both apps.
  `conversations/*/messages` still takes a participant's client delete from the builds that deleted a conversation from the client (store 2.1.x, and 2.2.0 built before this) — make it `allow delete: if false` once they're retired, which also ends either person deleting single messages.
- **Unsent messages** (round 16): unsending takes a message away for both people, but what it said is kept for moderation alone — `unsentMessages/{pair}_{id}` (rules: no client access; the picture it carried kept with it) — for `UNSENT_COPY_KEEP_MS` (30 days): a report of that conversation, or of one of its messages, filed while kept attaches the conversation's copies to its evidence (`unsent`, each `unsent: true`; an unsent message among the context is marked `unsent` too).
  The trash cron deletes copies past 30 days, oldest first, paged, each with its picture's files then record (`purgeUnsentCopies`; without the chat store the picture stays for the account purge); the account purge takes copies either way (`senderId` / `to`).
  The privacy policy says so (docs/legal).
  Notes can't be unsent (a letter is a record of reaching out); a reaction never lands on an unsent message (its transaction reads `unsentAt`).
- **私人卡 shared in a thread** (`src/lib/api/v1/sharedCards.ts`): an author may send their own 私人卡 (never published, or private) as a message's card; `sendMessage` marks that message `privateShare: true` (server-written; only for a 私人卡 at send time — a card sent while readable and made private later, or an older message's card, is shared with no one), and the marked message is its recipient's pass to read it through `GET /cards/{key}`, `?keys=` and a report only — never a note, resonance, bookmark or sending it on.
  Asked again on every read: unsending, 停止分享, deleting the conversation (and a message older than the conversation), a block either way, the trash, deletion, the card turning anonymous or published to readers all end it; those answers are `NEVER` cached.
  **停止分享** (`stopSharing`, `DELETE /api/v1/conversations/{pair}/shared-cards/{cardId}`, bucket `stopShare`, 100 a day): its author unmarks every message of theirs in that conversation sharing the card, in one transaction, at any age (unsend's 24 h don't apply); the messages stay, idempotent 204, across a block too, a conversation not theirs 404; sending the card again shares it again.
  Clients offer it in the held-message menu / ⋯ of the viewer's own delivered message with `privateShare` (`canStopSharing`), and draw someone else's 私人卡 on a message without the marker as a card the viewer can't read even when the thread read it earlier (`carriedOf`'s `viewerId`; the apps' twins).
  **Shared once, known for good**: `sendMessage` writes server-only `privateShares/{cardId}` = `{ authorId, at }` beside the marker, kept whatever becomes of the message (the recipient read it then) — deleted with the card (`deleteCard`) and the account (`collectAccountData`); its author asks `GET /api/v1/cards/{id}/private-shares` (`{ sharedPrivately }`, anyone else 404), and the publish step says `write.visibility.sharedWarning` under 匿名公開 while it is chosen (the web, iOS, Android).
  The owner's call: **warn, never refuse** — no rule or server path refuses such a card going anonymous (installed builds would only meet an error), and the card ⋯ offers no anonymity change of its own (公開發表 opens the publish step).
  An anonymous card no one else may read is refused (400); a draft's title never reaches `lastMessage`.
  Clients mark someone else's 私人卡 (`!isOwner` and private / unpublished) read-only: 🔒 私人卡 in the byline and `card.sharedPrivately` in place of the response row.
  The store builds up to iOS 2.2.0 (12) / Android 2.2.0 (14) would offer a note, resonance and bookmark there, so the routes read no pass for them (`knowsSharedCards` in `clientBuild.ts`: the card stays hidden, as before) — every later 2.2.0 build is iOS ≥ 13, Android ≥ 15; keep it so.
- **Reports**: filed through the server (`POST /api/v1/reports`, `…/cards/{key}/report`), which writes server-only `reportEvidence/{id}` in the same batch; read/resolve with `npm run moderation`. `reports/*` still takes a client create of a person or message report from the tester builds (no evidence then) — make it `create: false` once they're retired.
- **Who can reach whom**: two people message only once connected, and only the server connects them — through a resonance, a card's author answering a note, or an old invite its recipient accepts while it's open.
  Every path asks a pen name of whoever reaches out (`hasPenName` in `conversations.ts`: a profile with a non-empty `handle`; sending a message too) — before the blocks — and finds no block either way (on an anonymous card a block is never the answer, see above).
  Picking an anonymous card to resonate with reaches no one, so it needs no pen name.
  A resonance reaches its original (connection + bell) only when it is published, **public and named** (`reachable` in `resonate.ts`: a private or connections-only one is never listed under the original, an anonymous one would be named by the connection), through one transaction (`readReach` → `reachOriginal`) shared by publish, `POST …/resonances`, PATCH and edits/apply; the bell `notifications/resonance_{uid}_{originalId}` is the record — written once, and while it exists no bell is written and no connection made (between two already connected the resonance is still kept as a reason), so a reader rings a card's author once whichever path fires, and a connection a block ended isn't made again without a ring (an answered note still can).
  A bell from before the fixed id (`type: 'resonance'` under a random id, same `userId` / `payload.fromUserId` / `payload.cardId`) counts too: `readReach` looks for one with an equality-only query in the transaction while the fixed one is missing (single-field indexes merge; no composite index).
  Publish waits for the reach (best effort); PATCH and edits/apply run it after the response (`tryReachResonance` through `ringAfter`).
  An anonymous original rings but connects no one; an existing connection is never written over.
  For one day (2026-06-04/05, 5998ec4 → 90d9728) a "like" rang the very same row, and only its date tells the two apart (every such row from before 90d9728 went live was a like, but when that was is recorded nowhere), so a reader who liked a card that day counts as having rung its author: a resonance of theirs with it rings and connects no one; `scripts/integrity.ts` lists those rows.
  Why two people are connected lives in server-only `connectionOrigins/{pair}` (`origins.ts`; never on the connection, which both read, while a resonance can be made anonymous later): `reasons` — each standing resonance that reached, `resonance_{by}_{cardId}` (the one that connected them and each one after it while they were), `answered_{writer}` once the original's author of a standing resonance writes to its writer (a message, or a note in their thread: `answeredBy`, read against the reasons in that words' own transaction, so words from before a resonance never answer it and the resonator's own answer nothing; kept here, not judged from the thread, which either may delete), `letter` once a letter's recipient answered it, `invite_{id}`, and `legacy` for a connection made before reasons were kept (added with its first reason) — and `resonanceCards` to find them by.
  Only resonances are ever taken back; every other reason stands for good.
  A new connection writes it afresh (`connect`), so nothing of an earlier one a block ended counts.
  Taking a resonance back — `DELETE …/resonances/{cardId}`, deleting the card, or hiding it by PATCH or an applied edit (`hiddenResonance`) — removes its reason in the same transaction (`readTakeBack` → `takeBack`; deleting reads the card in that transaction, so a racing resonance can't outlive it), and the connection goes exactly when no reason is left.
  A connection made before reasons were kept is never taken back (its first reason comes with `legacy`).
  The bell stays too, so answering that card again rings and connects no one.
  Open with the owner: an original's author who deletes, hides or anonymizes their card keeps the connection (either of them can end it).
- **Resonating with a card — the tap** (共振, round 26, `lib/api/v1/taps`): kept on the server alone, `resonanceTaps/{cardId}_{uid}` (`{ cardId, userId, on, at, rangAt }`; the rules refuse every client read and write), read by card id — a shared-card pass reads none; on a draft, a trashed card and a named card across a block `CardDetail.tap` is null and `GET …/taps` 404.
  An author can't tap their own card (400: on an anonymous one their face would show to their connections); the weak absence inference that leaves (A10) is known and accepted, as for notes and replies with a card.
  Across a block a named card is 403 `blocked`; an anonymous card answers exactly as with no block — the tapper's answer, faces and `mayNote`, and what their connections see — and only the author's side (the faces, the list, the bell, the push) leaves the tapper out, after the response.
  A reader sees their own face first, then their connections' only (on a named card a block either way between the viewer and a face takes that face off; an anonymous card's faces skip the block filter, a block having ended the connection); the author sees the newest of everyone.
  A tap is safe: it connects no one, gives the author no way to message (their list opens a profile, unless that person left a note), taking it back tells no one, and the card shows no count (past its faces, `more` and a blank disc); only the author's bell row and push say how many others rang it that day (「和另外 {count} 人」, Push). It rings the author once ever per reader and card (`rangAt`, set only when it really rang, in a transaction after the response — never across a block or without a pen name), and only a PUT that turns it on rings: one made across a block stays unrung after the block is lifted, until its reader takes it back and taps again.
- **The passage picked after a tap** (round 27, `lib/cards/tapPassages`, `lib/api/v1/tapPassages`, `setTapPassage`): a card's tap passages are up to three sentences of its own story, word for word (`verifyPassages` drops anything that isn't a contiguous piece of the plain text), so they turn on the story alone and no probe of them names an anonymous author; they ride on `CardDetail.tap`, null wherever the reader may not tap. The pick (`passage: { id, text } | null` on the reader's `resonanceTaps` document) is learnt by the card's author alone — their list (`TapPerson.passage`); a reader's list carries only their own row's, never a connection's (`apiV1TapPassages.emulator.test.ts`). An anonymous card across a block takes, answers and shows the pick to the picker exactly as with none; only the author's list leaves them out (`anonymousBlocks.emulator.test.ts`); a named one across a block is 404. It goes wherever the tap goes (taken back → null; an anonymous card into the trash, `deleteCard`, the account purge either way); the export's `cardsResonated` rows carry the words on the condition `title` has. No number: nothing counts picks, the recommender reads none. `cards/{id}.tapPassages` is server-written (the rules' whitelists take it from no client, the edit's working copy included). An applied edit pays for them from the author's reindex budget, shared with the summary translations; out of budget, the daily cron makes them — a day at most, for a named and an anonymous card alike.
  An anonymous card going into the trash deletes its taps and its readers' reading records, as its notes go (`holdAnonymousCards` too); a restore brings neither back.
- **Reading records** (round 26, `POST /api/v1/cards/{id}/reading`, `lib/api/v1/reading`): `readerSignals/{uid}_{cardId}` (server-only, `expireAt` the last reading + 180 days, removed by the TTL policy) counts as `POST …/read` does — never the author, never a named card across a block, an anonymous card whatever the blocks — and the route answers any well-formed report 204 whatever the card (a bad id or body is 400 before any card is read), so nothing tells whether it was kept; no reader's name reaches an author, who sees only the weekly bell row of three readers or more (`read_summary`, public cards only; see Push). Clients send nothing for their own card, a private card or a shared-card pass (data-layer.md).
- **Notes are letters** (`sendNote` / `sendMessage` in `conversations.ts`): a note on a named card between two people not connected threads and rings like any note but connects no one; it waits in the conversation's `request` (server-only: the rules let a participant change nothing but their own unread) until its recipient answers — their first message, or a note of theirs back while it waits (crossing letters), connects the two and deletes `request` in the same transaction.
  Meanwhile its writer can leave 3 notes in all and can't message.
  The count lives in `letters/{writer}_{recipient}` (`{ from, to, count, at, cardId }`, server-only, closed to clients by the rules), not in the conversation, so either participant may delete the thread: the writer withdraws the letter, the recipient declines it — `request` goes with it, and the recipient's reply connects no one — and neither resets the count.
  Only its recipient answers a letter: their message or note to its writer, or an invite accepted between them, deletes `request` and both people's `letters/*` (`clearLetters`) — and, connected already, keeps the answered letter as a reason they are.
  A resonance connecting them leaves the letter as it is (clients ignore `request` while connected), so taking that resonance back leaves the letter and its cap exactly as before; the purge takes `letters/*` from either side.
  A block either way refuses the answer and leaves the letter as it is; a note on an anonymous card never opens, counts toward or answers one (a cap or a crossing there would tell whose card it is).
  People old notes connected stay connected.
- **Pen names**: `POST/PATCH /api/v1/me` reserve `handles/{lowercased name}` (server-only, one get allowed) in the profile's transaction and release it on rename into a hold — `handleHolds/{name}` (server-only, `HANDLE_HOLD_DAYS` = 90): its former holder may take it back, nobody else, so `/u/{old}` links never reach someone who took it at once; the account purge holds the last name for no one (no uid) before its reservation goes; lookups by pen name read the reservation (the client's only way: the rules list no `users`, since a list query, however small its pages, walks the whole directory).
  A profile without a pen name (made before onboarding asked for one) can reach no one: the web's `AppShell` sends it to `/signup`, and both apps send it to onboarding (as they do on GET /me's 404); either one's POST names it.
- **Account deletion**: `accountDeletions/{uid}` (admin-only) → purged by the cron.
  Scheduling it also moves the account's published anonymous cards to its trash, marked `trashedFor: 'deletion'` (`holdAnonymousCards`: private to everyone and answering as deleted from then on, their notes deleted as the trash deletes them, their pages revalidated then, never the profile's), and cancelling restores those alone (`releaseHeldCards`): an anonymous card ending in the purge's run beside its author's profile would say whose it was.
  A new collection that stores a uid must be added to `collectAccountData()` in `src/lib/account/deletion.ts` (it also takes the pen name's reservation and the holds naming them, the person's `uploads/*` pictures, the chat pictures they sent and the ones sent to them in their conversations (`chatImages/*` and their files, `deleteChatImageFile`), report evidence on either side, unsent messages' copies either way (`unsentMessages/*`), `letters/*` and `connectionOrigins/*` either way, their push switches and push logs (`notificationSettings`, `pickPushes`, `connectionCardPushes`), pending edits naming them, notes withheld from them (`withheldFor`), and what is found through one of their cards — legacy resonance records, every note left on it — just before that card).
  任務 #3 Phase 0 adds other people's blocks of them (`users/{other}/blocks/{uid}`, a collection-group query on `blockedUid` — its `COLLECTION_GROUP` field override in `firestore.indexes.json`; `accountPurgeBlocks.emulator.test.ts`); other people's bookmarks of their cards stay (`users/{other}/bookmarks/{cardId}` holds only the card's id, which goes under a tombstone; the shelf drops it).
  Round 26 adds, either way (`byCard`): `resonanceTaps/*` — the purged reader also taken off the `resonance_tap` bell rows that hold them (`purgeTapRows`) — and `readerSignals/*`; their cards' readings (`cardSignatures` by `authorId`, and `deleteCard` deletes `cardSignatures/{id}`); the export adds `cardsResonated` (the taps still on, a card's title only while they may still read it, linked by id) and `reading` (without the daily counts, with `keptUntil`).
  任務 #3 Phase 1 makes it registry-driven: every collection is an entry of `ENTITIES` in `src/server/domain/schema/registry.ts` (its person fields, how the purge takes it, its export section), the purge's queries and the export's sections are derived from it (`purgePlan()`, `EXPORT_SECTIONS`; the Firestore adapter `src/server/infra/firestore/repos/account.ts`, the services `src/server/app/account{,Export}.ts`), and `registry.test.ts` fails on a collection of the rules or the code that isn't registered, or a person field nobody deletes; `ports.account.emulator.test.ts` seeds every entity, purges, and finds the uid nowhere but in reports filed against the account.
  The cron purges two accounts at a time, 16 trees at a time each, through one BulkWriter, none for more than a quarter of the run (one cut short or failed is noted, `triedAt`, and waits behind every account due before that try), starts nothing new past its budget (logged `Account purge: out of time`; the rest waits for the next run) and deletes the profile last.
  `/api/account/export` streams compact JSON (bucket `export`).
  An account with an `apple.com` provider has its Sign in with Apple token revoked by the client just before it asks for the deletion (the request revokes the session the revocation needs; Firebase holds the web's Services ID and key, and there is no Admin SDK call for it): the web reauthenticates in Apple's popup and `revokeAccessToken`s the token it gets (`src/lib/auth/firebase/appleToken.ts`, only with `NEXT_PUBLIC_ENABLE_APPLE_SIGNIN`), iOS asks Apple's sheet for a fresh authorization code and `revokeToken(withAuthorizationCode:)`s it (`AppleTokenRevocation`, only when the device's Apple ID is the account's); closing Apple's window schedules nothing, any other failure lets the deletion go ahead.
  Android has no Sign in with Apple.
